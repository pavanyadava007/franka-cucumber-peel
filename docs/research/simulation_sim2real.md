# Simulating robotic food cutting, slicing, scraping and peeling, and its sim-to-real track record (for a Franka FR3/Panda cucumber peeling digital twin)

Research date: 2026-11-02. Scope: 2020 to Nov 2026. Venues marked "verified" were confirmed from arXiv comments, proceedings pages or project BibTeX. Venues not confirmed are marked "venue unverified". No numbers were invented: every number below is quoted from the cited page. Where a page summary disagreed with an abstract, both are reported.

## Q1. Has anyone published sim-to-real peeling specifically? If not, what is the closest evidence?

### Takeaway
I found no published work that trains a peeling policy (blade removing skin) in a physics simulator and transfers it to a real robot. Every real-robot peeling system I found (Kinova knife peeling 2026, MORPHeus ICRA 2024, ForceMimic ICRA 2025) learns or runs on real hardware with no simulation. The closest sim-to-real evidence comes from (a) the MIT/TRI vegetable-peeling system, which uses Isaac Gym sim-to-real only for the in-hand reorientation of the vegetable while a Franka holds the peeler, (b) sim-to-real food cutting (DiSECt, SliceIt!, RoboNinja, chicken deboning, DexNinja), and (c) sim-to-real thin-layer material removal analogues (FR3 sample scraping in MuJoCo with removable spheres, RoboFin3D grinding/sanding in Isaac Sim + Newton).

### Cited Findings

**Real-robot peeling (no simulation)**
- "How to Peel with a Knife" (Lin, Deng, Yin, Abbeel, Malik; arXiv 2603.03280, Mar 2026, venue unverified): Kinova Gen3 7-DoF torque-controlled arm with impedance control, a custom knife mount (knife, not a peeler), ATI mini45 F/T sensor streamed at 500 Hz, two RealSense D405 wrist cameras, SpaceMouse teleop data at 10 Hz. Diffusion Policy (ResNet-18 visual encoder, RGB + depth + 6-D wrench inputs), then a residual policy trained on a learned preference reward. Success = qualitative score > 3 on a 0-9 scale - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03280v1)
- Same paper: 100% in-category success on cucumber, apple and potato; cucumber policy 50% on zucchini, apple policy 90% on pear, potato policy 80% on daikon; cucumber with only 50 trajectories reached 100% success and mean score 8.5; uses 50-200 trajectories total - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03280v1). Note: the abstract claims "over 90%" success on out-of-distribution produce, which conflicts with the 50% cucumber-to-zucchini figure in the body - [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03280)
- Same paper uses no simulation; it says simulation-based cutting and sim-to-real "remain difficult to scale beyond simple insertion or cutting tasks" and lists "improved simulation and sim-to-real transfer methods" as future work to cut food waste. Failure modes: cutting too low, cutting too high, cross-produce generalization failures - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03280v1)
- MORPHeus (arXiv 2404.06570; ICRA 2024 per project BibTeX, verified): single arm plus assistive cutting board, multimodal active perception to decide whether an area is peeled, human-in-the-loop long-horizon planner for peel-coverage preference, compliant controller; 12 foods (carrot, daikon, cucumber, zucchini, eggplant, gold bar, acorn squash, apple, Chinese okra, Chinese yam, potato, radish). No simulation mentioned; robot model not named on the project page - [project page](https://emprise.cs.cornell.edu/morpheus); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06570)
- ForceMimic (arXiv 2410.07554; ICRA 2025 per arXiv comments, verified): zucchini peeling with a robot-free force-aware demo device (ForceCapture) and a hybrid force-position policy (HybridIL); +54.5% success relative to vision-only imitation learning; no simulation mentioned. Robot model not confirmed on the abstract page (a search summary said Flexiv Rizon 4; unverified) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07554)
- ForceFlow (arXiv 2605.11048) reportedly shows a cucumber peel holding normal force steady with a force-history flow-matching policy, qualitative only (from a search summary, not read in full; unverified) - [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.11048)

**Closest sim-to-real evidence in a peeling system**
- "Vegetable Peeling: A Case Study in Constrained Dexterous Manipulation" (Chen, Cousineau, Kuppuswamy, Agrawal; arXiv 2407.07884, Jul 2024, venue unverified): an Allegro hand on a Franka arm reorients the vegetable; a second Franka Panda holds the peeler (teleoperated or vision-based trajectory). The reorientation teacher is trained with RL in Isaac Gym and distilled to a proprioception-only Transformer student with DAgger. Peeling contact itself is NOT simulated - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07884v1)
- Same paper's sim-to-real recipe: gradient-free search to fit per-joint dynamics (friction, damping, max velocity, max effort) to real motors; domain randomisation of joint stiffness/damping, friction and restitution; random perturbation force on the object's centre of mass of 10x the object mass. Real results: with a 3.5 s command 90% of trials moved a surface point >= 4 cm (>= 7.3 cm at 7 s); lift success after reorientation 80-100% (10 trials each); failures mostly on small objects; Grounded SAM segmentation failures in vision-based peeling - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07884v1)

**Sim-to-real food cutting (per paper: venue, simulator, material model, robot, real results, code)**
- DiSECt (Heiden, Macklin, Narang, Fox, Garg, Ramos): RSS 2021 (verified, proceedings rss17 p067). FEM + SDF-based continuous contact + continuous damage model that "inserts springs on opposite sides of the cutting plane and allows them to weaken until zero stiffness"; calibrated to a commercial solver and real cutting datasets; Bayesian inference over hundreds of parameters - [RSS proceedings](https://roboticsproceedings.org/rss17/p067.html); [arXiv 2105.12244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.12244)
- DiSECt extended version (arXiv 2203.10263, journal venue unverified; a 2023 Autonomous Robots version could not be confirmed): real robot arm with slicing knife; parameters inferred from force measurements; optimized slicing motion reduced average knife force by more than 40% vs vertical cutting on fruit; code on project site - [arXiv 2203.10263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10263); [project](https://diff-cutting-sim.github.io)
- SliceIt! (Beltran-Hernandez, Erbetti, Hamaya; ICRA 2024 per arXiv comments, verified): dual simulator = DiSECt (cutting) + Gazebo (robot). Two UR5e arms with F/T sensors, Robotiq 85 holding the knife. Compliance via Forward Dynamics Compliance Control (FZI Cartesian controllers). SAC with a Temporal Convolutional Network over F/T history; the RL action is a reference trajectory plus stiffness Kc and PD gains Kp, Kd - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.02569v2)
- SliceIt! real2sim: "as few as one slice motion of each food item" (tomato, cucumber, potato) at constant knife speed; Optuna TPE search then Adam refinement over cut-spring stiffness [100, 8000], cut-spring softness [10, 5000], contact stiffness [200, 8000], contact damping [0.1, 100], friction stiffness [0.001, 8000], friction coefficient [0.45, 1.0]. Real tests: cucumber 15 slices at 5 mm, tomato 5, potato 10 at 3 mm, held-out carrot 5. Reports lower applied force than a Gazebo-only baseline; no success rates in text. Training ~40 h with dual sim vs ~4 h Gazebo-only. Code: github.com/omron-sinicx/sliceit - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.02569v2)
- RoboNinja (Xu et al.; RSS 2023 per authors' GitHub listing; DBLP not directly checked): differentiable Taichi MLS-MPM, elastoplastic with von Mises yield; core, knife and board as time-varying SDFs; Taichi autodiff with gradient checkpointing. Robot: UR5. Force sensing: strain-gauge load cell + HX711 at 80 Hz, about $8. Real: 3D-printed cores in Kinetic Sand, 8 in-distribution + 5 OOD geometries, completion 1.000, cut mass 0.877 / 0.824, collision ratio about 0.027-0.028 (non-adaptive baseline completion 0.125 / 0.200); real avocado/mango/plum qualitative only. Code via project page - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.11553); [project](https://roboninja.cs.columbia.edu/); [RSS 2023 listing](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Freal-stanford%2Froboninja)
- RoboCook (Shi, Xu, Clarke, Li, Wu; CoRL 2023, Best System Paper Award, verified on project page): no physics simulator; GNN dynamics on point clouds learned from about 20 minutes of real interaction per tool; 15 3D-printed tools on dough. Robot not named on the project page (often reported as Franka; unverified here). Code: github.com/hshi74/robocook - [project](https://hshi74.github.io/robocook/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14447)
- Chicken shoulder deboning (Yang, Hu, Ravichandar; arXiv 2510.15376; ICRA 2026 per arXiv comments, verified): open-source Python/Taichi simulator, MLS-MPM with CPIC, elastoplastic von Mises; knife as thin-cuboid SDF with Coulomb friction; sim scaled 5x for numerical stability. UR5 + Robotiq FT 300. Residual PPO (SB3, 2x64 tanh MLPs) on a single RTX 4090. Forces normalised and discretised to {0, 0.1, ..., 1.0} because "absolute contact dynamics are hard to model". DR = Gaussian noise on knife position N(0, 0.01) and orientation N(0, 0.1) - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15376v2)
- Chicken deboning results: sim success 0.80 vs nominal 0.20; physical testbed 0.75 vs 0.05; real chicken 0.50 vs 0.10 (0.70 vs 0.20 with bone cut < 0.05 g). Project website only, no direct repo link in paper - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15376v2)
- DexNinja (Lou et al., Tsinghua/INSAIT/others; arXiv 2610.17208, Oct 2026, venue unverified): real2sim2real dexterous slicing; custom differentiable simulator "based on RoboNinja and Warp", linear elastic materials; randomises mass, Coulomb friction, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, geometry and trajectory perturbations; meshes via Robo-GS, poses via FoundationPose. Real (100 trials, 4 foods): real-only 31% success, sim-only 34%, sim+real 50% (integrity 60%). Abstract's ">60% success" corresponds to the integrity figure, not success - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17208)
- Robot cutting via residual GP disturbance-force model (arXiv 2311.04096): milling process force model + residual GP learned from one or several real cutting-force examples; transfer "without the need for fine-tuning on the real robot" - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04096)
- Neural-stylisation sim-to-real for cutting unknown materials (arXiv 2601.20846; published in Scientific Reports 2026 per DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-41735-5): synthesises realistic training trajectories from unpaired real data with a VAE - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20846)
- Periodic rock-chop via virtual model control (arXiv 2508.02604): Franka, five vegetables, sub-millimetre slice accuracy for 1-7 mm slices at nearly one cut per second; a controller, not sim-trained - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02604)
- Sim-only cutting simulators (no real robot reported in abstracts): BladeMaster, GPU total-Lagrangian MPM with persistent cut labels, "faster-than-real-time" (arXiv 2609.27342) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27342); CulinaryCut-VLAP, MLS-MPM with forces from particle-grid impulse exchange plus a VLA dataset (arXiv 2601.06451) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06451); MG-CD-MPM continuum-damage MPM with two-way tool forces and PPO on cutting (arXiv 2610.39975) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39975)

**Analogous thin-layer removal / peeling domains**
- Sample scraping with FR3 (Cetin, Pouli, Pizzuto; arXiv 2603.10979, submitted to IROS 2026): MuJoCo; FR3 + scraping tool; material = several hundred spheres, each with a Perlin-noise dislodgement force threshold; Cartesian impedance at 500 Hz, physics 1 kHz; PPO (SB3); action = desired x-force, y-torque, z-position at 10 Hz; DR of joint friction, hardness thresholds and material layout; zero-shot transfer: 75.3% average relative success vs 64.44% fixed-wrench baseline - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10979v1)
- RoboFin3D (arXiv 2609.37560, Sep 2026): Isaac Sim 6.1.0 + Newton; workpiece as SDF updated by Preston's law r = k * max(p - p_c, 0) from hydroelastic contact pressure; sanding as a surface field D = (K_P/h_c) * integral(p * v_slip) with coating removed at D = 1; 15 N force setpoint; calibrated grinding volume error 20.2% (calibration pass) and 26.7% (evaluation pass); sanding IoU 0.81 / 0.89; not yet real-time - [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.37560)
- Grinding: Cutting Sequence Diffuser trains only on simple simulation data and transfers to a real robot by restricting actions to small removal volumes to "reduce the reality gap" (arXiv 2412.14417) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14417); cutting-surface-aware model-based RL for grinding (arXiv 2308.02150) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02150)
- Tape peeling: TapeSim (arXiv 2609.28766) on Newton; on 100 paired Peel cases balanced accuracy improved from 50% to 72.9-76.3%; 3.2-3.4x physics-step speedups at 32 turns - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28766). Velcro peeling with recurrent Q-learning and tactile input, 90% success on a real robot (arXiv 2011.08319) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08319)
- Surgical: visual sim-to-real tissue retraction at 50% real success with pixel-level domain adaptation (arXiv 2406.06092) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06092); ORBIT-Surgical (Omniverse/Isaac) sim-to-real on a physical dVRK (arXiv 2404.16027) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16027); LapGym on SOFA with a "Dissection" track among 12 envs, sim-only baselines (arXiv 2302.09606) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09606); SimRetract zero-shot 90% grasp / 78% retraction held at handoff on phantoms (arXiv 2610.38234) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38234); CRESSim-MPM GPU MPM for cutting/suturing in Unity (arXiv 2502.18437) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18437)

### Inferences
- A sim-to-real cucumber peeler on an FR3 would be a novel contribution: the gap sits between "real-only peeling" (Kinova, MORPHeus, ForceMimic) and "sim-to-real cutting/scraping" (SliceIt!, deboning, FR3 scraping).
- The most transferable template for peeling is the FR3 scraping paper (same robot family, MuJoCo, impedance low level, removable-particle material with force thresholds, zero-shot), combined with SliceIt!'s idea of the RL policy outputting impedance gains and the deboning paper's discretised force observations.
- Real-only peeling reached 100% in-category with 50 cucumber demos, so a sim-trained policy will be judged against a strong real-data baseline; sim is most defensible for safety, food-waste reduction, coverage planning, and DR-driven robustness.

### Gaps
- No paper found that simulates a peeler blade lifting a skin strip off a cylinder; I could not verify whether any thesis or workshop paper exists.
- Robot model for MORPHeus and RoboCook not confirmed on the pages read (commonly reported as Kinova and Franka respectively; unverified).
- DiSECt journal venue and RoboNinja proceedings page not directly opened.

## Q2. Which simulator is the best fit for a cucumber peeling digital twin on a GTX 1650 4 GB locally or an NVIDIA L4 24 GB in the cloud?

### Takeaway
For a GTX 1650, MuJoCo (CPU, plus MuJoCo Warp/Newton on GPU) with a rigid cucumber and a custom skin-removal layer is the practical choice; Isaac Sim is not supported on a GPU without RT cores and lists 16 GB VRAM as minimum. On an L4, Isaac Lab 3.x (PhysX FEM or Newton) becomes possible, and Genesis or a Warp/Taichi MPM is the route if true elastoplastic skin cutting is needed. No mainstream simulator offers "blade removes thin skin layer" out of the box; MPM (Genesis, Newton, Taichi, Warp) is the only family with native material separation, while MuJoCo flex and Isaac FEM deform but do not fracture.

### Cited Findings
- Isaac Sim requirements (page updated 14 Oct 2026, references Isaac Sim 6.0): GPU minimum GeForce RTX 4080, good RTX 5080, ideal RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell; VRAM 16 / 16 / 48 GB; "GPUs without RT Cores (A100, H100) are not supported"; Linux driver 595.58.03. L4 is not named on this page - [Isaac Sim requirements](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/latest/installation/requirements.html)
- Isaac Lab 3.0 deformables: PhysX simulates soft bodies with FEM; the experimental Newton backend uses VBD-based deformables from isaaclab_contrib; backends listed as physx, newton_vbd, ovphysx; tutorial cube E = 1e5, Poisson 0.4, density 500, Newton VBD at 10 iterations and 4 substeps; the Newton VBD path skips the simulation-mesh collider - [Isaac Lab deformable tutorial](https://isaac-sim.github.io/IsaacLab/release/3.0.0/source/tutorials/01_assets/run_deformable_object.html); [migration notes](https://isaac-sim.github.io/IsaacLab/release/3.0.0-beta2/source/migration/migrating_deformables.html)
- Newton: "initiated by Disney Research, Google DeepMind, and NVIDIA", a Linux Foundation project, Apache-2.0; "integrates MuJoCo Warp as its primary backend"; MPM examples (granular, two-way coupling, snow, dam break), VBD, cloth (incl. ### Inferences
- GTX 1650 (Turing TU117, compute capability 7.5, no RT cores, 4 GB) sits exactly at Warp 1.18's floor (CC 7.5, driver R580+), so Newton/MuJoCo Warp should run, but Isaac Sim is excluded (no RT cores, below 16 GB). Fallback on older drivers: warp-lang 1.17.0. These specs are standard NVIDIA facts, not from the cited pages.
- Recommended split: develop and debug in MuJoCo (CPU, Menagerie franka_fr3) with a rigid cucumber and a custom skin layer (Q3); scale RL on the L4 with MuJoCo Warp/Newton or MJX. Use Isaac Lab on the L4 only if photoreal rendering or Isaac-specific assets matter; L4 has RT cores and 24 GB, but the requirements page does not list it, so treat L4 support as unverified.
- Use MPM (Genesis, Newton MPM, or a RoboNinja-style Taichi/Warp solver) only as a high-fidelity "teacher" or calibration reference for a few hundred rollouts, not for mass RL on a 4 GB card; MPM particle counts for a cucumber-sized object plus thin skin will be memory-heavy (no measured number found).
- MuJoCo flex and Isaac FEM/VBD can give a compliant cucumber body but cannot remove skin by themselves; removal must be bookkept in user code either way.

### Gaps
- No benchmark found of MPM or FEM peeling/cutting throughput on a 4 GB GPU or on an L4.
- No official statement found on Isaac Sim support for L4 in this pass; ManiSkill3's current shipped soft-body (MPM) GPU support not confirmed beyond the paper text.
- Genesis per-solver differentiability and VRAM floor not documented on the pages read.

## Q3. What is the minimum-viable peel model that still gives useful policies, and which signals must be matched to real for transfer?

### Takeaway
Published sim-to-real material removal that worked used simple, calibrated removal rules on top of rigid or compliant contact, not full fracture physics: removable spheres with force thresholds (FR3 scraping), an SDF updated by a Preston-type pressure rule (RoboFin3D), and small per-step removal volumes (grinding). The minimum-viable cucumber peel model is therefore: rigid (or lightly compliant) cucumber, skin as a removable surface grid or particle layer, a removal rule driven by blade normal force/pressure, contact depth and blade angle, plus coverage bookkeeping, with domain randomisation of geometry, friction, stiffness and removal thresholds. The signals to match are wrench (normal and tangential), blade pose/angle, depth of engagement and stroke speed, under the same impedance controller as the real FR3.

### Cited Findings
- Removable-particle layer that transferred zero-shot on an FR3: spheres with per-sphere dislodgement force thresholds from Perlin noise; policy observes end-effector pose, velocity magnitude, estimated external wrench and residue % per material cluster; acts in hybrid force/position space (x-force, y-torque, z-position) at 10 Hz over a 500 Hz impedance controller - [arXiv HTML 2603.10979](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10979v1)
- Height/SDF removal rule calibrated to real: grinding r = k * max(p - p_c, 0) on an SDF; sanding coverage field D accumulating pressure x slip speed, removed at D = 1; k fit to first-pass removed volume, contact stiffness and p_c fit to height profiles; residual volume error 20-27% - [arXiv HTML 2609.37560](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.37560)
- Restricting each step to small removal volumes reduced the reality gap enough for real grinding transfer - [arXiv 2412.14417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14417)
- Real peeling quality metric used in practice: peel thickness from wrist-camera data in six discrete categories, plus a 0-9 human score; failures are "cutting too low" and "cutting too high" (i.e., depth control) - [arXiv HTML 2603.03280](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03280v1)
- Real peeling coverage: MORPHeus uses active perception to classify peeled vs unpeeled areas and a planner for coverage preference - [project page](https://emprise.cs.cornell.edu/morpheus)
- Force as the key input: ForceMimic's hybrid force-position primitive gave +54.5% over vision-only on zucchini peeling - [arXiv 2410.07554](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07554); the knife-peeling policy takes the 6-D wrench as input - [arXiv HTML 2603.03280](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03280v1)
- Calibrating contact stiffness, damping, friction and cut-spring parameters from a single real slice per food was enough for SliceIt! to train lower-force cutting policies - [arXiv HTML 2404.02569](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.02569v2)
- Matching relative, not absolute, force: normalised and discretised force observations narrowed the force gap in deboning transfer - [arXiv HTML 2510.15376](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15376v2)
- Letting RL output impedance parameters (stiffness, PD gains) alongside the trajectory - [arXiv HTML 2404.02569](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.02569v2); learning residual compliance parameters online from F/T after DR-trained offline RL - [arXiv 2310.10509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10509)
- Domain randomisation dimensions that were used for food/contact tasks: mass, friction, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, geometry, trajectory offsets/approach angles (DexNinja) - [arXiv HTML 2610.17208](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17208); joint friction and material hardness thresholds (FR3 scraping) - [arXiv HTML 2603.10979](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10979v1); joint stiffness/damping, friction, restitution, object perturbation forces (vegetable reorientation) - [arXiv HTML 2407.07884](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07884v1)

### Inferences
- Proposed minimum-viable cucumber model (synthesis, untested): rigid capsule or scanned mesh cucumber clamped like MORPHeus' board; skin as a 2-D grid over (axial z, angle theta) storing remaining skin thickness; a peeler blade modelled as a rigid edge with MuJoCo soft contact; each step, for cells under the blade, remove thickness when normal force exceeds a threshold and blade angle is inside a "bite" window, with removal depth tied to penetration depth; penalise removal below skin thickness (flesh gouge = "too low"), and count skipped cells ("too high"). Coverage = fraction of cells at zero thickness.
- Randomise radius, taper/curvature (bent cucumbers), surface friction, contact stiffness, per-cell removal thresholds (Perlin noise as in the scraping paper), clamp pose and cucumber pose.
- Match on the real FR3: (1) normal force during a stroke, (2) tangential drag per stroke, (3) blade tilt relative to the local surface normal, (4) engagement depth/peel thickness, (5) stroke speed. Calibrate thresholds and k from a handful of real strokes with an F/T sensor, mirroring SliceIt!'s one-slice calibration and RoboFin3D's first-pass calibration.
- Run the same Cartesian impedance law in sim as on the robot and keep the policy at about 10 Hz, as the scraping paper did, so the policy is isolated from high-frequency contact dynamics.

### Gaps
- No published cucumber-skin mechanical parameters (skin thickness, peel force vs blade angle) were found in this pass; these must be measured, not assumed.
- No paper compares a height-field removal model against MPM for peeling-like tasks, so the fidelity loss of the shortcut is unquantified.

## Q4. What sim-to-real techniques work for contact-rich force tasks, and what real success has been reported after transfer?

### Takeaway
The techniques with reported real success are: dynamics/contact domain randomisation with force-aware policies (FORGE, FR3 scraping), system identification of joint dynamics (vegetable reorientation), residual RL on a nominal trajectory (deboning: 0.50 vs 0.10 on real chicken), simulator calibration from a few real force traces (SliceIt!, DiSECt), sim pretraining plus real data co-training (DexNinja 50% vs 31% real-only; co-training +38% average), and real-to-sim reconstruction with Gaussian splatting or spring-mass twins (PhysTwin, real2sim-eval, GaussTwin on FR3). Pure zero-shot transfer works best when the policy acts through an impedance/force interface and sees normalised force.

### Cited Findings
- FORGE: force-threshold mechanism + dynamics randomisation; policies conditioned on a maximum allowable force; predicts task success; snap-fit insertion and planetary-gear assembly (arXiv 2408.04587) - [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04587). Robot not named in the abstract (commonly Franka; unverified here)
- CoRMA reports that FORGE baselines "achieve high simulation success but degrade substantially on hardware" on a Marvin arm in Isaac Lab / Isaac Sim 5.0 - [arXiv 2605.22082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22082)
- IndustReal: simulation-aware policy updates, SDF rewards, sampling-based curricula and a policy-level action integrator for sim-to-real assembly - [arXiv 2305.17110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17110)
- Residual RL: deboning residual PPO with discretised forces, zero-shot; real chicken success 0.50 vs nominal 0.10 - [arXiv HTML 2510.15376](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15376v2); TRANSIC learns residual policies from human corrections for contact-rich sim-to-real - [arXiv 2405.10315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10315)
- Sim pretraining + real fine-tuning: SGFT uses a sim value function to guide real exploration on five dexterous tasks where zero-shot fails - [arXiv 2502.02705](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02705); sim-and-real co-training improves real performance by an average of 38% - [arXiv 2503.24361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24361); DexNinja sim+real 50% vs real-only 31% for slicing - [arXiv HTML 2610.17208](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17208)
- System identification: gradient-free fit of joint friction, damping, max velocity and effort before Isaac Gym training (vegetable peeling reorientation) - [arXiv HTML 2407.07884](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07884v1); task-driven simulator adaptation (AdaptSim) incl. dynamic food scooping with a spatula, about 2x real data efficiency vs Sys-ID - [arXiv 2302.04903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04903)
- FR3 + MuJoCo MJX sampling MPC real-to-sim-to-real (Push-T): online DR of contact-initiation parameters gave interpretable signals, global physics parameters were "too weak for reliable exploitation" at replanning rates - [arXiv 2606.20712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20712)
- Real-to-sim digital twins: PhysTwin (spring-mass + generative shape + Gaussian splats from sparse videos) - [arXiv 2503.17973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17973); Gaussian-splat soft-body twins whose simulated rollouts "correlate strongly with real-world execution" for policy evaluation - [arXiv 2511.04665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04665); GaussTwin (PBD + Cosserat rods + splats) tested on an FR3 - [arXiv 2603.05108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05108); differentiable MuJoCo + splats for end-to-end real-to-sim on ALOHA 2 - [arXiv 2506.04120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04120); RoboGSim real2sim2real - [arXiv 2411.11839](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11839)
- Surgical deformable sim-to-real: 50% real retraction success with pixel-level domain adaptation, no retraining - [arXiv 2406.06092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06092)

### Inferences
- For an FR3 peeler, a practical stack is: identify FR3 joint friction/damping (or randomise them, as the scraping paper did), keep libfranka-style Cartesian impedance in the loop, train a residual or force-conditioned policy over a nominal axial stroke, randomise contact and removal parameters, then co-train or fine-tune with a small real dataset (the knife-peeling work shows 50 real cucumber demos already suffice for a strong baseline).
- Gaussian-splat twins mainly close the visual gap and help policy evaluation; they do not by themselves model skin removal, so they would sit on top of the Q3 removal model.

### Gaps
- No FR3-specific published impedance/sysid parameter set was retrieved in this pass (pre-2020 Panda identification work exists but was out of date range and not fetched).
- FORGE and IndustReal real success numbers were not read from full text, so none are quoted.

## Q5. What are the documented failure modes of food-cutting (and related) sim-to-real?

### Takeaway
Documented failures cluster into: material-response mismatch (blade stalling, fruit compression, stiffness), friction and unmodelled static friction, absolute force magnitude mismatch, tool compliance (knife bending), sensing limits (1-DoF force, per-material thresholds), fracture/topology artefacts and numerical instability, compute cost of high-fidelity cutting sims, and perception/registration errors. Skin-specific issues (fibrous skins, high opening force) showed up in real RoboNinja tests.

### Cited Findings
- Cutting-physics mismatch was 17 of 50 failed sim+real DexNinja trials (blade stalling or fruit compression "not captured by simulation"); grasp/contact control 19, perception/registration 8, kinematic/safety 6 - [arXiv HTML 2610.17208](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17208)
- Friction: an explicit force penalty learned in sim caused oscillations that failed on hardware due to unmodelled static friction (FR3 scraping) - [arXiv HTML 2603.10979](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10979v1)
- Absolute contact dynamics hard to model, so forces were normalised/discretised; force normalisation needs manually collected maximum values; sim scaled 5x for numerical stability (deboning) - [arXiv HTML 2510.15376](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15376v2)
- Knife bending in the real world shifted apparent collision positions; a 1-DoF force sensor misses rotation energy; force thresholds needed per-material tuning; mango skin's initial opening force exceeded the robot's limit; fibrous skins (mango, plum) needed an extra back-and-forth slicing primitive; raw meat too tough, cooked meat used (RoboNinja) - [arXiv HTML 2302.11553](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.11553)
- Robot simulator (Gazebo) cannot model in-plane cutting forces, only normal contact; dual-sim training cost about 40 h vs 4 h; generalisation shown on one unseen food (SliceIt!) - [arXiv HTML 2404.02569](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.02569v2)
- Removal-model error: 20.2-26.7% removed-volume error after calibration; simulated exposed area 11-16.5% larger than measured (RoboFin3D) - [arXiv HTML 2609.37560](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.37560)
- Peeling-specific real failures: depth errors (too low / too high) and cross-produce generalisation failures - [arXiv HTML 2603.03280](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03280v1); small vegetables slipping from a blind (proprioception-only) grasp, segmentation failures - [arXiv HTML 2407.07884](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07884v1)
- Simulator artefacts reported by users: abnormal MPM cutting and failed MPM grasping in Genesis examples - [Genesis issue 351](https://github.com/genesis-embodied-ai/genesis-world/issues/351); [issue 1640](https://github.com/genesis-embodied-ai/genesis-world/issues/1640)
- Sim success not carrying over to hardware for force-conditioned assembly policies - [CoRMA arXiv 2605.22082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22082)
- Topology-changing cuts are where "small errors in contact, friction, or material and elasticity responses quickly compound" - [DexNinja arXiv 2610.17208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17208)

### Inferences
- For cucumber peeling, the likely top risks are: skin removal threshold vs real peel force (stiffness/toughness mismatch), surface friction and peeler-edge drag, cucumber curvature/taper and clamp compliance, and wrist F/T noise. Design the sim so the policy relies on relative force trends and blade geometry, not exact force magnitudes.
- Peeler blades are short and stiff compared with a chef's knife, so the knife-bending failure seen in RoboNinja is probably smaller, but peeler mount compliance should still be checked on the real FR3.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of how much each mismatch (stiffness, friction, geometry) degrades peeling transfer exists, because no sim-to-real peeling study exists.
- Real cucumber skin variability (ripeness, waxing, temperature) vs peel force was not found in robotics literature in this pass.
